Unit 4 Source Review and Access Record

The assigned written sources were accessed and analyzed individually. Their roles differ: the textbook explains adaptive project management; the vendor sources describe delivery practices and platforms; the prototype readings clarify how an early implementation can support feedback. The reflection report cites only sources that directly support its claims. The two videos could not be reviewed in full audiovisual form or through a complete verified transcript. That limitation is recorded below rather than treating descriptions as a complete viewing.

Oguz and Agile project management

Oguz (2025), Chapter 12, sections 12.0 through 12.5, connects adaptive development to uncertainty, iterative planning, customer feedback, and organizational change. The sections on environment and Scrum explain that an Agile process depends on responsibilities and collaboration as well as events and artifacts. The trend discussion does not make one framework universally appropriate. For this Capstone, short increments and reviewable evidence are useful; a one-student project should not claim that using Git and a backlog constitutes a fully staffed Scrum team. CI checks provide feedback within an iteration but do not replace stakeholder validation.

Parzych and the curated DevOps readings

Parzych (2019) presents eight resources spanning DevOps fundamentals, measurement, pipeline tools, automated acceptance testing, production testing, and Agile practices. Its useful theme is the interaction between people, process, tooling, and continuing feedback. It is a curated 2019 guide, not current comparative evidence about platform performance. I use it for the cultural argument and verify contemporary platform mechanisms in the platforms' own documentation. Its discussion of production testing also needs context: safeguards are necessary, and production experimentation is outside this research prototype's scope.

Dell handbook

Narasimhan et al. (2023) describe the lifecycle from planning and coding through building, testing, integration, deployment, operation, and monitoring. The handbook connects automation with collaboration and discusses benefits, adoption costs, security, and future directions. It is a vendor white paper, so its general benefits are not proof that this Capstone will achieve any particular improvement. The actual PDF carries a 2023 copyright date. The assigned MERLOT record was added or updated in 2025; October 15, 2025 is a catalog-record date, not a verified publication date for the PDF. The reflection therefore uses 2023.

Red Hat and DevOps

Red Hat (2025) identifies culture, automation, platform design, and feedback as core aspects of DevOps. Its sections on infrastructure as code, shared accountability, adoption challenges, and DevSecOps are especially relevant. Security checks belong inside the delivery process, and operational responsibilities must be clear. The page also promotes Red Hat products; those promotional sections do not establish a need to adopt OpenShift or Kubernetes. A local Python application and modest CI workflow fit this project's scope more closely than introducing infrastructure without a research purpose.

Laptick and the prototype model

Laptick (2022) explains the sequence of requirements gathering, quick design, prototype construction, review, and refinement, and distinguishes different prototyping approaches. The useful design lesson is that a working example can uncover unclear expectations earlier than a specification alone. For this project, a gate and evidence preview make withholding behavior inspectable. The danger is to mistake a demonstrable interface for a finished implementation. The temporary lexical signal and extractive response are therefore labeled explicitly, with learned detection and real LLM evaluation retained as future work.

GeeksforGeeks additional reading

GeeksforGeeks (2025) provides a supplementary explanation of prototyping phases and feedback-driven refinement, including advantages and limitations. It reinforces the distinction between a prototype and a finished system. Because this is a general educational summary and the same point is supported by the assigned Laptick reading, it is not needed in the reflection report's reference list. It contributes background rather than evidence for detector performance or CI/CD platform capabilities.

The Knowledge Academy video

The assigned YouTube URL for The Knowledge Academy (2023) failed to expose playable content or a complete transcript through the available retrieval route. Online searches located the catalog description and related material by the same provider, but those are not substitutes for the exact video. The accessible description identifies Azure DevOps as a platform for collaborative development and deployment. I make no scene-level or quotation claims about the recording. The report's Azure Pipelines description instead uses Microsoft (2026), whose page displays an update date of June 22, 2026. The two video references below retain the dates supplied in the assigned reading list.

Simplilearn video

The Simplilearn (2021) video page and indexed metadata were found, but a complete verified transcript and audiovisual playback were unavailable. Indexed chapter summaries describe the separation between development and operations and a lifecycle involving planning, coding, building, testing, deployment, and monitoring. Those summaries were used only to identify the video's introductory role; they are not treated as independently verified details of the presentation. The report's substantive claims use the accessible textbook, handbook, and official platform pages. Neither video is cited in the report as if it had been fully watched.

Overall application

The sources support an iterative route from a small demonstrable implementation to repeatable tests and controlled delivery. They do not establish that passing CI implies adversarial robustness. The current package consequently separates local software checks from future research evaluation. Official GitHub, Jenkins, GitLab, and Microsoft documentation was used to verify platform mechanisms; a separate Red Hat CI/CD explanation verifies the distinction between continuous delivery and continuous deployment. These platform sources are cited in full in the reflection report.
